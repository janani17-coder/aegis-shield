# Aura Workforce – Staff Security Awareness Guide

**Audience:** All staff and contractors  
**Purpose:** Help protect company accounts, employee information and business systems.

## 1. Recognise and avoid phishing

Phishing is a message designed to trick you into revealing information, opening an unsafe attachment or approving an unexpected request.

**Warning signs**
- Unexpected messages asking you to act urgently.
- Links that do not match the organisation's genuine website.
- Requests for passwords, MFA codes or confidential information.
- Unexpected invoices, payroll changes or document-sharing invitations.
- Messages from familiar names but unusual email addresses or writing styles.

**What you should do**
1. Pause before clicking a link or opening an attachment.
2. Check the sender's full email address, not just the display name.
3. Verify unusual payment, payroll or account requests using a separate trusted channel.
4. Do not reply with passwords, MFA codes or confidential company information.
5. If uncertain, report the message to the security team before acting.

Never assume a message is genuine just because it uses a company logo or knows your name.

## 2. Use strong, unique passwords

A password protects access to company information. Reusing one password across different services increases risk.

**Good password habits**
- Use a long, unique password or passphrase for every work account.
- Use an organisation-approved password manager where available.
- Never share passwords with colleagues or support staff.
- Do not store passwords in plain text, shared documents or messages.
- Change a password immediately if you believe it has been exposed.
- Use only approved password-reset processes.

IT or security staff should never ask you to disclose your current password.

## 3. Protect accounts with MFA

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) adds another verification step when signing in. It helps protect an account even if its password is compromised.

**MFA safety**
- Enable MFA wherever the organisation supports or requires it.
- Prefer approved authenticator apps or security keys where available.
- Never approve an unexpected sign-in request.
- Never share one-time codes, recovery codes or push approval requests.
- If you receive an MFA prompt you did not initiate, deny it and report it.
- Contact the approved IT support channel if you lose access to your MFA method.

An unexpected MFA prompt may mean someone is attempting to access your account.

## 4. Handle company information carefully

- Access only the information required for your role.
- Share files only with authorised recipients through approved services.
- Check recipient addresses before sending payroll or employee information.
- Lock your screen when leaving your workstation.
- Keep devices and software updated through approved processes.
- Do not move company information to personal email, personal storage or unapproved applications.
- Follow the organisation's data classification and handling policies.

## 5. Report suspicious activity immediately

Report a concern even if you are unsure whether it is a real incident. Early reporting helps the organisation investigate.

**Report examples**
- A suspicious email, link or attachment.
- An unexpected MFA request or sign-in alert.
- A lost device or suspected account compromise.
- An accidental disclosure or misdirected email.
- Unusual access to employee, payroll or confidential records.
- Unexpected changes to account or payment details.

**How to report**
1. Stop interacting with the suspicious message, link or activity.
2. Do not delete relevant messages or evidence unless instructed by the response team.
3. Contact your manager and the organisation's designated IT/security reporting channel immediately.
4. Include the time, affected account or device, what happened and any actions already taken.
5. Follow instructions from the authorised response team.

Do not investigate the incident yourself or forward suspicious attachments to other colleagues.

**If you clicked a suspicious link or shared information:** report it immediately. Do not hide the mistake. Prompt reporting is more important than blame.

## 6. Staff security checklist

- [ ] I use unique passwords and approved password storage.
- [ ] I protect MFA codes and reject unexpected approval requests.
- [ ] I check suspicious messages before acting.
- [ ] I share company information only through approved channels.
- [ ] I lock my device when away.
- [ ] I know how to contact the designated IT/security reporting channel.
- [ ] I report suspected incidents promptly.

## Remember

**Stop. Check. Verify. Report.**

Security is everyone's responsibility. Report concerns promptly and use approved company procedures.

*This guide is a project awareness document for the fictional Aura Workforce environment. Before operational use, management must publish the real reporting contact, escalation route and approved support channels.*

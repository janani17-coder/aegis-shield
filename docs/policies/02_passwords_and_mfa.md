# Policy 2: Passwords & Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

**Policy ID:** POL-02  
**Owner:** Security Team  
**Status:** Draft

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how to protect user accounts with strong passwords and an additional identity check when signing in. These measures help prevent unauthorized people from accessing company systems and information.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, administrators, and approved users who sign in to company accounts, applications, or systems. It covers password creation and use, additional identity checks during sign-in, and the protection of account credentials.

## 3. Rules

1. Administrator accounts and accounts used to access systems remotely must use multi-factor authentication (MFA). MFA means proving your identity using an additional step beyond your password, such as an approved authentication app.
2. Passwords must contain at least 15 characters when a password is the only method used to verify identity.
3. Systems must block passwords that are commonly used, known to have been exposed in a data breach, or easy to guess.
4. Users must never share their passwords or use their work passwords for personal accounts.
5. Users must store passwords in an organization-approved password manager when one is available. A password manager is a tool that securely stores passwords.
6. Systems must limit repeated failed sign-in attempts and alert the responsible team when the configured limit is reached.
7. Users must change their passwords promptly if they suspect, or confirm, that someone else knows them. Passwords do not need to be changed on a fixed schedule unless a security risk or applicable standard requires it.
8. Recovery codes and requests to reset sign-in verification must be protected. The user's identity must be verified before account access is restored.
9. Default passwords supplied with a new system or account must be changed before the system or account is used.
10. IT or Security staff must review sign-in security alerts and investigate suspicious activity according to the organization's incident response process.

## 4. Roles

- **All users:** Create strong passwords, keep them private, use approved additional sign-in verification when required, and report suspected account compromise.
- **Managers:** Ensure team members understand and follow this policy.
- **IT team:** Configure sign-in protections, manage account recovery securely, and maintain approved password-management options.
- **Security team:** Review sign-in alerts, investigate suspicious activity, and coordinate responses according to the incident response process.
- **System owners:** Ensure systems block weak passwords, limit repeated failed sign-in attempts, and change default passwords before use.

## 5. Exceptions

Any request for an exception to this policy must be documented and approved by the designated manager and IT/Security team before the exception is applied. The request must explain why the exception is needed, the risks involved, and how those risks will be reduced. Exceptions must have an expiry or review date.

Users who cannot meet a password or sign-in requirement must contact their manager or IT/Security team for help. They must not disable security protections or bypass the rules themselves. Alternative sign-in methods may be used only when approved by IT/Security.

# Facilitator Guide - Payroll Data Breach Tabletop Exercise

**Project:** Aegis Shield Cybersecurity Assessment

**Playbook:** AS-15 - Payroll Data Breach

**Exercise Type:** Planned human-led tabletop exercise

**Status:** PREPARED - NOT YET CONDUCTED

## 1. Exercise Objective

Evaluate whether participants understand their incident-response responsibilities when unusual payroll export activity is detected.

The exercise is discussion-based. No production systems, real employee information or live incident-response actions are involved.

## 2. Participants and Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Facilitator | Introduces scenario updates and records discussion |
| Incident Response Lead | Coordinates incident response and escalation |
| SOC Analyst | Reviews alerts and identifies required evidence |
| Payroll System Owner | Confirms business authorization and approves appropriate system actions |
| IT Security Representative | Advises on evidence preservation and security controls |
| Privacy or Legal Representative | Reviews notification obligations, where available |

Record actual participant names and roles only when the exercise takes place.

## 3. Scenario

A PAY-02 Full Payroll Export alert is generated outside normal business hours.

The alert indicates unusual payroll export activity. Authorization and the extent of any data exposure have not yet been confirmed.

No real payroll or employee information is involved.

## 4. Exercise Ground Rules

1. Discuss decisions; do not perform actions on real systems.
2. Do not assume an alert proves a confirmed breach.
3. Identify who has authority to approve decisions.
4. Explain what evidence is needed to support each decision.
5. Record unresolved questions and control gaps.
6. Do not record fictional answers as actual participant responses.

## 5. Facilitator Discussion Questions

### Stage 1 - Detection and Validation

- What information should the SOC analyst record?
- Which logs and records should be reviewed?
- Who should receive the initial escalation?
- What evidence is needed before classifying the event?

### Stage 2 - Authorization Review

- How should the team verify whether the export was approved?
- Which official approval records should be checked?
- Who can confirm the business purpose?

### Stage 3 - Containment Decision

- Who has authority to approve an access restriction?
- Which team is authorized to carry out an approved restriction?
- How should the decision and its timestamp be documented?

### Stage 4 - Impact and Notification

- How will the team establish the possible scope of exposure?
- Who assesses privacy and legal notification requirements?
- How should uncertainty be communicated?

### Stage 5 - Recovery and Lessons Learned

- What evidence is needed before normal activity resumes?
- How will payroll data integrity be reviewed?
- What corrective actions should be assigned and tracked?

## 6. Evidence Checklist

- Original PAY-02 alert and timestamp
- Relevant authentication records
- Payroll audit records
- Official export approval records
- Access-permission records
- Incident escalation record
- Decision and approval history
- Evidence handling register
- Post-exercise improvement action log

## 7. Exercise Evaluation

The facilitator should assess whether participants:

- Identified the correct escalation roles.
- Distinguished suspicious activity from a confirmed breach.
- Identified appropriate authorization evidence.
- Recognized approval requirements for containment.
- Identified privacy and legal review responsibilities.
- Documented evidence gaps and follow-up actions.
